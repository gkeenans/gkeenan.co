In the nearly two months since I last published something on this very good blog, Katy and I:

* Listed our house.
* Got two offers on our house in less than two weeks.
* Sold our house.

I can't believe it's actually happening. Can't believe how fortunate we've been. 

Like, truly, I feel like an asshole because of how all of this is shaking out the way it is? The way that everything seems to have fallen into place. How it all seems to just. Be. Working. I don't even like mentioning it, because, again, asshole feelings. I don't want to brag! I'm not a braggart!![^1]

But it also doesn't feel right to, you know, *not mention it.* To refuse to take note of just how magical and momentous and unusual this all feels. To dismiss our fortuitousness in an impish display of nonchalance, pretending as though The Universe hasn't just *kicked open the goddamned door and is now vehemently ushering us through it.* "¡Vámonos! Los geht's! Dawaj! Move your fuckin' ass!" Says The Universe. Do you brush off *THE UNIVERSE* as though it's coming to you with the dessert menu and you're all, *Oh, please, no! I truly couldn't. I'm so full. I couldn't possib—oh, but they do have a crème brûlée...*

No. We're really, really lucky to be in this position. And when an opportunity like this presents itself, it feels absurd to turn it down. I've lived too much of my life trying to cling to what feels comfortable, and it's never been worth it. The best experiences I've had, the most I've grown as a person, have been when I've taken the leap, regardless of how scary it is.

And that's why we've been saying to each other from the beginning, when Katy first submitted her dual-citizenship application: "If it happens... Do we go?" And the consensus has always been: "We'd rather go and regret it, than stay and regret not knowing." 

So when she got the letter from Austria, congratulating her on her citizenship, we said it's time. And when she received her EU passport, we said it's really real. And when her job said they'd be willing to transfer her to Warsaw, we said we're actually doing this. And when we sold our house, we said holy shit we just sold our house. And when we were doing a test pack of all of the things we want to bring with us, we said fuck we're going to need more boxes. Even though I was pretty sure I ordered enough boxes, I didn't order enough boxes. We're ready to part ways with most of our earthly possessions, but we still need more boxes. Fine. Whatever. We'll figure it out.

And then in less than three weeks I'll leave everything I've known behind. Three weeks from now I'll be in the middle of the ocean, a ship ferrying me to a new continent. A new life. A plethora of new experiences. Something I've dreamed about for as long as I can remember, finally happening. A month-long excursion across Europe until I reunite with my wife and our dog in Berlin and we finish the last leg of our journey to Warsaw by train. I couldn't ask for a better way to transition from one chapter into another.

How did I get so lucky?

Katy and I keep saying to each other, to our friends, to our family, to really anyone who will listen: "We're just waiting for the other shoe to drop." We know this is going to be hard. We can't possibly prepare for everything. When will reality smack us in the face?

I know it will. I'm sure the first time I try and, I dunno, [buy lemons in our new home](https://gkeenan.co/avgb/lmao-almost-started-crying-about-polish-7-eleven-this-morning/) my world will come crashing down. Not to mention applying for residence. Or a PESEL number. Or getting a cell phone plan. Or a bank account. Or or or or or.

But I have to believe we'll figure it out, even though, as things become more real, I am less confident in my ability to navigate it. But I'm here in this moment having navigated everything for 40 years. So it wouldn't be completely unheard of that, just maybe, when I look back at this period a year from now, five years from now, twenty years from now, I'll think, "Ha ha, how quaint. You had no idea what was ahead of you." Or, "Holy shit that was five years ago!" Or, "I never could've anticipated that we'd someday be living on the Moooooooooooooooooooon!"[^2] 

Or, at the very least: "We learned a lot." The overwhelm doesn't last forever.

I can't wait. Can't wait to sit across from Katy at a cafe, sippin' on a latte or something, and say to her, "Did you have any idea we'd be here now?" And we'll both laugh and talk about how we didn't anticipate any of this. Ten years ago, we were just friends. Six years ago, we had just married (and were now sheltering in place—*unrelated*). Four years ago, we were just about to buy our first house and move from Chicago to Indiana. Two years ago, talking about leaving the US was like asking, "What should we do if we win the lottery?"

Three weeks from now, I'll be on my way to something I can't even imagine. Not really. It's all just little ephemeral specks that dissipate the second I latch onto them. They won't be tangible until they're here. I won't know what they meant to me until they're gone. But I have to believe that, someday in the not-too-distant future, I'll be looking at my wife sitting in a chair in our little, like, glass bubble greenhouse moonbase thing, like from the 50s or whatever, thinking about how lucky I am. How I couldn't have ever seen this coming. And how much easier it was because we knew on the other side of it all, that at least we'd be here together, ready for the next adventure.
