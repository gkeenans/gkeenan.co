I caved and spent the $32 fucking dollars to have 24 hours of internet access in the middle of our 10 straight days at sea. I might as well post about it.

Unfortunately, all I have are the ramblings I’ve written in my journal during my voyage. So I suppose I’ll transcribe those and clean them up a bit.

### Day One

Leaving Port Miami was strangely anticlimactic. I waved goodbye at the land strangers like you do. Like everyone do. And I watched as we slowly distanced ourself from the city, but at a certain point I got distracted by exploring the ship and didn’t pay attention to the shrinking coastline. The next thing I know, we’re out on the open water and everything was gone.

It wasn’t until a few hours later when the reality of being out on the ocean truly hit me. I noticed my anxiety slowly dialing up with each gentle rock of the ship. It continued to swell throughout the evening, reaching a fever pitch as I sat down at the steakhouse for dinner. It almost completely obliterated my appetite. By the time I made it to my room, I was ready to cry. I popped a propranolol and texted Katy and Halsted. By the time I said goodnight, the propranolol had settled my heart and I eventually began to appreciate the omnipresent sway. The rhythmic creak of my cabin wall.

I then realized I would not be able to sleep just yet, so I went upstairs to get a late-night slice of pizza and look at the stars. I could not see many, because the ship’s lights were so bright.

### Day Two

This morning I woke up before sunrise. I put on some comfy clothes and made my way up to the top deck to watch the sun crest over the horizon. I stood and stared at the ocean stretched out before me for 30 minutes until the golden crest appeared behind a line of clouds. It was one of the most rejuvenating experiences of my life.

### Day Three

The sea is so impossibly vast. So impossibly blue. The camera doesn’t capture it accurately, in scale or aesthetic. It is remarkable to witness. I wasn’t expecting to feel like this, but I understand the allure now. Yesterday, I laid down in a canopy chair and watched the sea and the sky and the clouds for an hour and a half. We are on our second full day at sea, with seven more to go before Liverpool, and I have a hard time believing that I will ever tire of it.

I find I get surprisingly giddy when I spot a vessel on the water. The first night, I saw another cruise ship in the distance, numerous decks adorned in light, a beacon of celebration shining on the horizon. Other vessels are few and far between, but every ship is a reminder of others out there, journeying to somewhere. All of us just tiny specks drifting amongst an impossible expanse. What do the barges carry, I wonder. Teddy bears? Oil? Condoms?[^1] 

I spoke with one of the deck hands, Killare, just now. We talked on the first day as well, right before leaving Miami, so it was nice to chat with him again. He’s from India and says he loves his work. Loves the opportunity it affords him. Seeing the world. When I mentioned I was moving to Poland when we first met (he asked why I was sailing), his eyes lit up. He told me about a Polish woman he met in his village when he was 11 or 12. She helped open his eyes to the world and encouraged him to explore. He said she was the reason he was eager to take this job. Even though he met her once years ago, her words still linger with him, and he thinks often about how she affected the trajectory of his life. 

He’s such a kind person and I am so grateful he was willing to share a part of his experience with me.

*[Later, back in my cabin]*

An experience I keep having, especially in the moments where I look out at the sea, is that it dawns on me that I am here. This is happening. It is all real. I, like, keep snapping back into reality. I said goodbye to everyone. Everything I’ve known. I had so many lasts that I didn’t consider. The last time I charged the car. The last time I walked in our favorite parks. The last Chick-Fil-A, or Taco Bell, or Popeye’s. There will be plenty of food, especially good food, I’m sure. But I think it’s realizing the loss of ubiquity. Will I miss fuckin’ *Taco Bell?* I dunno, but *Taco Bell* is something I’ve known all my life.

So, yeah, it’s strange looking out at the ocean and thinking about Taco Bell, which then balloons into bigger and bigger reality checks. Where you stop and recognize *Oh! I am here. In this moment! Holy shit!* Because while this adventure is producing its fair share of these moments (seemingly on an hourly basis) they are not exclusive to this vacation. I often would sit at home, after we bought our house, and think: *Holy shit. We own a house!* Or after Katy and I got married: “Holy shit! We’re married!” We exclaim this to each other often, and it always makes me smile. Like, how wonderful to be lost in your own thoughts or routine and get to snap out of it and be delighted at your impossible fortune?

### Day Four

The persistent wiggle of the light fixtures is disconcerting. Even moreso than the relentless rocking of the ship. The rocking feels purposeful. A machine performing its duty with deliberation. The wiggle feels chaotic, as though the structure of the ship is wholly compromised. The chandeliers are the canary in the coal mine. They tremble with every shudder of the vessel. They betray the quiet confidence of the rest of the ship’s construction. They validate the unnerving sensation of rumbling I perceive almost every moment I am still. But the truth is that I’m never still, and neither are the lights. We’re always here, shaken to our core. 

I wonder how many others on board think about the lights. Would they if they peeled their eyes away form their phones for even five seconds? The glassy-eyed, vacant stares of those who wander the halls would suggest probably not. It’s remarkable to me just how many people here are simply wobbling up and down the length of the ship, seemingly oblivious to the world around them. Mournful spirits haunting a battlefield. When their gaze does stray in your direction, they stare right through you.

Maybe *I’m* the ghost. I certainly feel like I have unfinished business. I’m on a mission, but currently trapped in limbo. 10 more days of this. 22 more days until I am made whole again. When I can hug Katy and this time be damn well sure that I never let go.

I wonder if this “vacation” would feel more like a vacation if it wasn’t also a means to an end. If at the end of this journey a new life didn’t await us. Could I revel in this if the entire purpose was to relax? I kept saying it was, but I kinda feel like I’ve been lying to myself.

Maybe I just don’t love traveling solo? I’ve been trying to keep an open mind, but I can’t shake the longing for someone to share this with. I don’t mind being alone with my thoughts, but I vastly prefer the company of Katy’s.

### Day Five

It’s May 28. I’m not writing this in my journal, instead typing directly into my computer. I bought the internet package today, and the second I did, I found myself stuck in the familiar pattern of checking my messages and email and Mastodon and Reddit. I’ve barely looked at the sea. I feel it more acutely in my body though. It’s making me ever-so-slightly nauseous today, and I have a hard time believing that’s a coincidence. It’s hard to regulate when your body sways and your eyes are transfixed on a screen.

I don’t love how quickly the routine set in, and I am glad I didn’t buy the internet for the entire journey. I don’t think I would have spent the time journaling, or reading, or watching, or just *existing*. Technology is incredible. I am grateful for what it affords me, especially when I feel so far away and lonely. But I also resent it. I felt it before I left. I feel it even more now.

I just looked out my cabin window and got distracted by the waves. Holy shit. I’m here. This is happening. This is real. I can’t believe how fortunate I am to be here to experience it.
